## 1. Executive Summary & Purpose

CineVerse operates an omni-channel digital movie ticketing platform, connecting film patrons with exhibition theatres across India (Telangana, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Delhi NCR, Tamil Nadu, and West Bengal). 

To ensure complete statutory compliance under the Central Goods and Services Tax (CGST) Act 2017, Integrated Goods and Services Tax (IGST) Act 2017, and respective State GST acts, this document details the architectural decisions and statutory determinations requiring explicit written guidance from our Chartered Accountant.

## 2. Open Decisions for Chartered Accountant Sign-off

### Question 1: Principal vs. Agent Supplier Model for Admission Tickets
* **Current Placeholder Implementation:** Cinema is configured as the legal supplier (`ticket_supplier_model = "CINEMA"`). The cinema's legal entity, GSTIN, and registered address appear as the Supplier on the Ticket Tax Invoice. CineVerse acts strictly as an electronic booking facilitator.
* **Alternative Consideration:** CineVerse as Principal under an inventory/merchant model (`ticket_supplier_model = "PLATFORM"`), where CineVerse buys admission capacity and re-sells to customers.
* **Statutory Reference:** Section 2(5) (definition of agent), Section 2(17) (business), and Section 7 of the CGST Act.
* **Specific CA Input Needed:**
  1. Does our standard Cinema Exhibition Agreement classify CineVerse as an "agent" collecting money on behalf of the cinema, or does it trigger Section 9(5) liability?
  2. Should the admission ticket invoice be issued under the Cinema's GSTIN with CineVerse named as the Electronic Commerce Operator (ECO), or directly by the Cinema?

### Question 2: E-Commerce Operator (ECO) Classification: Section 9(5) vs. Section 52 TCS
* **Current Placeholder Implementation:** Tax Collected at Source (TCS) is currently disabled (`tcs_applicable = False`). CineVerse collects the customer payment via payment gateway, retains its convenience fee, and remits the ticket + F&B proceeds net of gateway costs to the cinema partner.
* **Statutory Reference:** 
  - **Section 9(5) of the CGST Act:** Notification No. 17/2017-Central Tax (Rate) specifies certain services where the ECO is liable to pay tax as if it were the supplier (e.g., passenger transport, accommodation, housekeeping, restaurant services).
  - **Section 52 of the CGST Act:** Every ECO not covered under Section 9(5) who collects consideration on behalf of suppliers is required to collect TCS @ 1% (0.5% CGST + 0.5% SGST or 1% IGST) of the net value of taxable supplies.
* **Specific CA Input Needed:**
  1. Do movie exhibition services fall under Section 9(5)? *(Standard industry consensus: Cinema admission is NOT notified under Sec 9(5), meaning the cinema remains the taxable person).* Please confirm whether any recent notification or tribunal circular has altered this position.
  2. If Section 52 applies, is CineVerse required to register for TCS in every state where cinemas are located, and deduct 1% TCS on monthly remittances to cinema exhibitors?

### Question 4: Place of Supply (POS) Determination (B2C vs. B2B)
* **Current System Implementation:**
  - **Tickets & F&B:** Under Section 12(6) of the IGST Act, the Place of Supply for admission to an entertainment event or cinema hall is the place where the event is actually held (i.e., the location of the Cinema). Therefore, Ticket and F&B supplies are almost always **Intra-State** (CGST + SGST) relative to the cinema's registered state.
  - **Convenience Fee:** For registered B2B patrons providing a verified GSTIN, POS is the registered location of the recipient (Section 12(2)(a)). For unregistered B2C patrons, POS defaults to the address on record / location of the supplier (Telangana, 36).
* **Specific CA Input Needed:**
  1. For cross-state patrons (e.g. a customer residing in Bengaluru, Karnataka booking a ticket for a cinema in Hyderabad, Telangana), is the convenience fee treated as an Inter-State supply (IGST 18% from CineVerse Telangana to Karnataka) or Intra-State supply based on the cinema's location?
  2. Does the admission ticket supply under Section 12(6) strictly prohibit IGST even if a corporate B2B buyer from another state books bulk tickets?

### Question 5: Invoicing Architecture: Split vs. Consolidated Invoices
* **Current Implementation:** Split Dual Invoicing:
  1. **Invoice 1 (Cinema Tax Invoice):** Issued by the Cinema for Movie Admission (and F&B if ordered), showing Cinema GSTIN as Supplier.
  2. **Invoice 2 (CineVerse Platform Invoice):** Issued by CineVerse Digital Pvt Ltd for Convenience Fee, showing CineVerse Telangana GSTIN as Supplier.
* **Alternative Consideration:** Single Consolidated Invoice issued by CineVerse with separate disclosure of the Cinema's tax and CineVerse's fee.
* **Specific CA Input Needed:**
  1. Under Rule 46 of the CGST Rules, is split invoicing legally safer for avoiding commingling of cinema turnover with platform turnover?
  2. Does split invoicing create any friction with Rule 54(4A) regarding ticket as tax invoice?

### Question 7: Credit Note Issuance & Tax Reversal on Cancellations/Refunds
* **Current System Implementation:** 
  - When a booking is cancelled within the cinema's policy window (75% ticket refund, 100% addon refund, non-refundable convenience fee), the engine automatically issues a sequential **Credit Note** linked to the original Tax Invoice (`original_invoice_id`).
  - Output tax liabilities are reversed proportionally in integer paise.
* **Statutory Reference:** Section 34 of the CGST Act.
* **Specific CA Input Needed:**
  1. Section 34(2) requires credit notes for a financial year to be declared no later than the 30th day of November following the end of the financial year, or the date of furnishing of the annual return, whichever is earlier. Does our automated credit note engine satisfy all required statutory fields (original invoice number, date, taxable value, and tax rate reversed)?
  2. If the convenience fee is retained as non-refundable upon cancellation, no credit note is issued for Invoice 2. Please confirm if this is the correct tax treatment.
